# Facts — "राज्य कसरी चल्छ? कानुन कसरी बन्छ?" (भित्र के हुन्छ? विशेष)
Prepared 2026-11-14. Constitution of Nepal (2015/2072) as amended; descriptive only, no party, person or current dispute named.

## Organs (Constitution of Nepal)
Text source used: nepallaws.com reproduction of the Constitution's English translation (cross-check against Nepal Law Commission official text before publishing).
- Art. 75(1): "The executive power of Nepal shall ... be vested in the Council of Ministers." 75(3): federal executive functions performed in the name of the Government of Nepal.
- President is the head of state (Art. 61) — standard constitutional fact (not re-fetched in this session).
- Art. 84(1)/86 via IFES factsheet (2016): HoR 275 (165 FPTP + 110 PR), 5-year term; National Assembly 59 (56 elected, 8 per province; 3 nominated by President on Government recommendation).
- Courts: Supreme Court (Art. 128), High Courts (Art. 139), District Courts (Art. 148) — three tiers.
- Art. 133(1): a citizen may petition the Supreme Court "to have any law or any part thereof declared void on the ground of inconsistency with this Constitution".
- Three levels of state (federal, province, local) with province assemblies and village/municipal assemblies — standard constitutional fact (Art. 56; not re-fetched).

## Law-making (Constitution, Part 9)
- Art. 110(1): Bill may be introduced in either House; Money Bill only in the House of Representatives. 110(2): Money Bills (and bills on security bodies) only as Government Bills.
- Art. 111: passed bill goes to the other House; National Assembly returns Money Bill within 15 days, other bills within 2 months; disagreement → back to originating House; joint sitting in specified cases (111(9)).
- Art. 113: Speaker/Chairperson of the originating House certifies and submits; President assents within 15 days; non-Money Bill may be returned once for reconsideration; "A Bill shall become an Act after the President gives assent to it."
- Art. 114: Ordinance only when both Houses are not in session, on Council of Ministers' recommendation; same force as an Act; ceases if not passed by both Houses, and in any case at the expiry of 60 days after both Houses meet.
